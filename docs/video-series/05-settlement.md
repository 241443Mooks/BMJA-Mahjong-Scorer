# Video 05 — Settle the Four Scores

**Working title:** How Settlement Works in British Mahjong: Who Pays Whom?

**Series context:** this video uses the exact four scores built in Videos 01–04.

| Wind | Outcome | Hand score |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| East | non-winner | 56 |
| South | **winner** | **60** |
| West | non-winner | 40 |
| North | non-winner | 44 |

Prevailing Wind is East. South wins.

## Core lesson

Scoring the four hands is only the first part. British Mahjong then settles the hand pairwise:

1. every loser pays the winner the winner's score;
2. the three non-winners settle the differences between their own hand scores;
3. any payment involving East is doubled.

The hand score and the settlement change are therefore **not the same thing**.

## Canonical settlement

### Winner payments

South won with 60.

- East → South: base 60 × East multiplier 2 = **120**
- West → South = **60**
- North → South = **60**

South receives **240** at this stage.

### Non-winner differences

East 56 vs West 40:

- difference = 16
- West pays East
- East is involved, so the payment doubles
- West → East = **32**

East 56 vs North 44:

- difference = 12
- North pays East
- East is involved, so the payment doubles
- North → East = **24**

North 44 vs West 40:

- difference = 4
- West pays North
- East is not involved
- West → North = **4**

### Net settlement changes

| Wind | Net change |
| --- | ---: |
| South | **+240** |
| East | **-64** |
| West | **-96** |
| North | **-80** |

Zero-sum check:

`240 - 64 - 96 - 80 = 0`

## Shot order and narration

### 1. Recap the four hand scores

Show the full game scorer with the four scores entered and South marked as winner.

> Over the last four videos we scored each player separately. South went Mah Jong with 60. East scored 56, West scored 40 and North scored 44.

> In British Mahjong, that still is not the end of the hand. We now settle those scores between the four players.

### 2. Explain winner payments

Focus on South as winner.

> First, every losing player pays the winner the winner's score. South's score is 60, so West pays South 60 and North pays South 60.

> East also owes South 60, but any payment involving East is doubled. So East pays South 120.

> That means South receives 240 from the winner-payment stage.

### 3. Explain that losers also settle with each other

> The three losing players are not finished yet. They also compare their own hand scores and pay the differences between them.

### 4. East vs West

> East scored 56 and West scored 40, a difference of 16. West would normally pay East 16, but East is involved, so that doubles to 32.

### 5. East vs North

> East scored 56 and North scored 44, a difference of 12. North pays East, and again East doubles the payment, so North pays 24.

### 6. North vs West

> North scored 44 and West scored 40. The difference is only 4. East is not involved this time, so West pays North 4.

### 7. Show net changes

Focus on the app's settlement explanation / net changes.

> Once all of those payments are combined, South is up 240. East is down 64. West is down 96. North is down 80.

> Those changes add to zero, because settlement only moves points between the four players.

### 8. Main conceptual close

> This is why a British Mahjong hand score and a player's settlement change are not the same thing. East actually had the highest losing hand at 56, but East's payments are doubled whenever East is involved.

> The scorer handles both stages: first the hand scores, then the settlement between the table.
