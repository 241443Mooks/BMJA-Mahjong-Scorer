# Video 02 — East: Scoring a Partial Losing Hand

**Working title:** How to Score a Losing Hand in British Mahjong

**Series context:** same four-player hand as Video 01. South has gone Mah Jong. East is a non-winner. Prevailing Wind is East.

**Target score:** **56**

## Canonical entered evidence

- player Wind: **East**
- prevailing Wind: **East**
- non-winner
- partial evidence only
- exposed Pung of East Winds = 4 points
- exposed Kong of 6 Bamboo = 8 points
- pair of Red Dragons = 2 points
- own-Wind Pung = 1 double
- prevailing-Wind Pung = 1 double

Calculation:

`4 + 8 + 2 = 14 points`

`14 × 4 = 56`

## Core lesson

A losing player does **not** need to reconstruct all 13 structural tiles if they only want to score the completed evidence they know.

The scorer should show this as a legitimate **partial hand**, not an error.

## Shot order and narration

### 1. Set context

Set player Wind to East, prevailing Wind to East, non-winner.

> South has already gone Mah Jong, so East has not completed a winning hand. We only need to enter the parts of East's hand that actually score. The scorer can treat that as partial evidence rather than forcing us to invent the rest of the tiles.

### 2. Add exposed Pung of East Winds

> This is an exposed Pung of East Winds. Winds are major tiles, so an exposed major Pung is worth 4 points.

> This Pung is also especially useful for showing context. East is this player's own Wind, and East is also the prevailing Wind. A Pung or Kong of your own Wind gives one double, and a Pung or Kong of the prevailing Wind gives another. So this one set gives two separate doubles.

### 3. Add exposed Kong of 6 Bamboo

> Next is an exposed Kong of 6 Bamboo. Six Bamboo is a minor tile. An exposed minor Kong is worth 8 points.

> A Kong has four physical tiles, but it still represents one completed set in the hand.

### 4. Add pair of Red Dragons

> Finally, East has a pair of Red Dragons. A pair of Dragons scores 2 points.

### 5. Show partial-hand status

> We have not entered every tile East was holding, and we do not need to. These completed scoring elements give us 14 points. Because the evidence is partial, the scorer does not try to invent whole-hand patterns or fishing from tiles it has not seen.

### 6. Apply doubles

> The East-Wind Pung gives two doubles: one because it is East's own Wind and one because East is the prevailing Wind.

> Fourteen doubled twice is 56.

### 7. Close

> So East's hand scores 56 from the evidence we actually know. In the next videos we will score West and North, then bring all four scores together and settle the hand.
