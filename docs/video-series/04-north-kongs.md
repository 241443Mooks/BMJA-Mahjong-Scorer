# Video 04 — North: How Kongs Score

**Working title:** How Kongs Score in British Mahjong

**Series context:** same four-player hand. South has gone Mah Jong. North is a non-winner. Prevailing Wind is East.

**Target score:** **44**

## Canonical entered evidence

- player Wind: **North**
- prevailing Wind: **East**
- non-winner
- partial evidence
- concealed Kong of 7 Characters = 16 points
- exposed Pung of Green Dragons = 4 points + 1 double
- pair of North Winds = 2 points

Calculation:

`16 + 4 + 2 = 22 points`

`22 × 2 = 44`

## Core lesson

Kongs score much more than Pungs. A Kong contains four physical tiles but still counts as one completed set in the hand structure.

## Shot order and narration

### 1. Set context

Set player Wind to North, prevailing Wind to East, non-winner.

> North is our final player. This hand is useful because it contains a Kong, which is scored differently from the Pungs we have already seen.

### 2. Add concealed Kong of 7 Characters

> This is a concealed Kong of 7 Characters. Seven is a minor tile. An exposed minor Kong would score 8 points, but a concealed minor Kong scores 16.

> Although a Kong contains four physical tiles, it still represents one completed set in the hand.

### 3. Add exposed Pung of Green Dragons

> North also has an exposed Pung of Green Dragons. Dragons are major tiles, so the Pung scores 4 points.

> And, just as we saw with South's Red Dragons, any Dragon Pung or Kong gives one double.

### 4. Add pair of North Winds

> Finally, this is a pair of North Winds. Because North is the player's own Wind, the pair scores 2 points.

### 5. Show calculation

> Sixteen for the Kong, 4 for the Dragon Pung and 2 for the own-Wind pair gives 22 basic points.

> The Green Dragon Pung gives one double, so 22 doubled once is 44.

### 6. Series bridge

> That gives us all four hand scores: South won with 60, East scored 56, West scored 40 and North scored 44.

> But that still is not the end of scoring a British Mahjong hand. The final stage is settlement: working out who pays whom. That is what we will do next.
