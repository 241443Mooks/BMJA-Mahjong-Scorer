## Purpose

This document turns live search evidence into a bounded acquisition strategy for Mahjong Reference.

It does **not** replace the Table Companion product direction. The distinction is deliberate:

- **Product positioning:** Mahjong Reference is the table companion for the whole game.
- **Acquisition language:** people search for concrete jobs such as `mahjong scoring calculator`, `mahjong hand calculator`, `British mahjong rules`, `mahjong game tracker`, `mahjong settlement`, special hands and rules comparisons.

Core principle:

> **Search for the problem. Arrive at the table companion.**

The growth opportunity is broader than a British-only scorer. Mahjong is the market; fragmented rules, scoring, settlement, tracking and variant identification create the discoverable jobs that Mahjong Reference can solve unusually well.

## Priority acquisition clusters

### 1. Calculator / scorer cluster — first priority

Current evidence already shows page-one visibility for:

- mahjong calculator;
- mahjong scoring calculator;
- mahjong scorer;
- mahjong point calculator;
- mahjong hand calculator;
- mahjong hand score calculator;
- mahjong hand builder.

This is the fastest likely acquisition win because the product directly satisfies the query.

Required discipline:

- `/` owns the broad scoring-calculator job;
- `/hand` owns the one-hand job;
- `/game` owns full-game tracking;
- titles/H1s/descriptions/internal links should make those boundaries obvious;
- do not make every route compete for `mahjong calculator`.

The homepage may use concrete search language such as **free Mahjong scoring calculator** without replacing the product proposition **Your Mahjong table companion**.

### 2. British rules/scoring cluster — defend the beachhead

Current British visibility is already strong:

- `british mahjong scoring` ~3.5;
- `british mahjong rules` ~8.2;
- `british mahjong tiles` ~8;
- `british mahjong` ~12.

British/BMJA-style content is the easiest authority beachhead and should remain explicit even as the product expands globally.

Do not flatten British pages into generic Mahjong pages merely to appear broader.

### 3. Rules comparison and identification — strategic expansion

`/mahjong-rules-compared` is already being tested for pairwise searches including Riichi/Hong Kong and Hong Kong/American comparisons.

This supports the existing product direction for a **Which Mahjong rules do I play?** diagnostic.

The funnel should become:

```text
variant/comparison search
    -> useful comparison or diagnostic
    -> identify likely rules family/profile
    -> rules/reference page
    -> correct scorer/game entry
```

Create pairwise comparison pages only where they answer a genuinely distinct question. They should not be templated doorway pages.

Useful candidate themes to validate with search data:

- Riichi vs Hong Kong Mahjong;
- Hong Kong vs American Mahjong;
- British vs Hong Kong/Chinese Mahjong;
- British vs Western/Classical Mahjong;
- American vs British Mahjong;
- why different Mahjong groups score/play differently;
- what type/version of Mahjong does my family/club play?

The diagnostic should allow uncertainty (`I'm not sure`) and use the fewest discriminating questions practical.

### 4. Special hands — scalable long-tail authority

`/special-hands` is already discovered for generic and named-hand searches.

Potential future structure:

```text
/special-hands
/hands/wriggling-snake
/hands/triple-knitting
/hands/gates-of-heaven
/hands/buried-treasure
...
```

Each individual hand page must earn its existence by being useful, for example:

- visual/structural explanation;
- how to recognise the hand;
- required tiles/shape;
- rules profiles that recognise it;
- scoring/value by supported rules profile;
- common confusions;
- source/provenance where relevant;
- direct route into the scorer where the profile supports it.

The multi-profile architecture creates a defensible advantage: answer **whether and how the hand exists in the rules the visitor actually plays**, rather than publishing generic copied definitions.

### 5. Full-game tracking and settlement — product moat

Issue #116 already defines the acquisition model for:

- mahjong game tracker;
- mahjong score tracker;
- scorekeeper/running score;
- full-game scoring;
- settlement;
- who pays whom / who owes what.

Keep **Table Companion** as the proposition while targeting the vocabulary people actually search.

This cluster matters strategically even if its raw search volume is lower than generic scoring because it maps to the product's harder-to-copy capability and future account/Plus value such as saved games, history and preferences.

### 6. Additional rules profiles — expand only when truthful

As supported profiles become public, repeat the same job-based model rather than creating generic SEO clones:

- `[ruleset] rules`;
- `[ruleset] scoring`;
- `[ruleset] scoring calculator` where the product actually supports it;
- `[ruleset] special hands/patterns` where relevant;
- `[ruleset A] vs [ruleset B]` when the comparison has real user value.

Never imply scorer support merely because a reference page exists.

## Internal-link model

Use descriptive, varied anchors and connect acquisition content to a useful next action.

```text
Rules / comparison / special-hand content
            ↓
        correct scorer
       ↙            ↘
 one-hand scorer   full-game tracker
       ↘            ↙
          settlement
```

Good anchor concepts include:

- score this Mahjong hand;
- Mahjong hand calculator;
- track a full Mahjong game;
- Mahjong settlement explained;
- compare Mahjong rules;
- find which Mahjong rules you play;
- British Mahjong scoring guide.

Avoid repetitive exact-match anchor spam.

## Search-content quality rule

Do not manufacture SEO pages that the product cannot uniquely support.

Every acquisition page should do at least one of:

- answer a real rules/scoring question better;
- visualise/clarify a Mahjong concept;
- identify a rules context;
- provide a worked, test-backed example;
- route directly into a relevant interactive tool;
- expose profile-aware differences competitors commonly flatten together.

No hidden search-only copy, doorway pages or mass-generated near-duplicates.

## Guardrails

- Product truth beats keyword opportunity.
- Rules provenance beats convenient generic copy.
- Do not present British/BMJA-style rules as universal Mahjong rules.
- Do not imply official BMJA affiliation.
- Do not claim full Western verification while #121 remains open.
- Do not create a competing `/mahjong-game-tracker` while `/game` owns that job.
- Do not create `/rules/compare` while `/mahjong-rules-compared` owns comparison.
- Do not move established URLs merely for taxonomy neatness.
- Do not let `Table Companion` positioning prevent the site from using concrete acquisition language such as calculator, scorer, tracker and settlement.
- Do not change deterministic rules/scoring behaviour to fit SEO copy.

## Strategic thesis

The near-term beachhead is British Mahjong scoring because Mahjong Reference is already receiving page-one tests there.

The expansion path is horizontal rather than abandoning that niche:

```text
British scoring
    -> calculator/scorer jobs
    -> rules comparison and identification
    -> special-hand reference
    -> full-game tracking and settlement
    -> additional supported rules profiles
```

This lets a small, high-confidence niche establish search authority while the product grows toward a much larger Mahjong-wide surface.

> **Mahjong is the market. Rules fragmentation is the opportunity.**
