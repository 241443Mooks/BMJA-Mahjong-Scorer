# Video 01 — How British Mahjong Scoring Works

**Working title:** How British Mahjong Scoring Works: Points, Doubles & Your Final Score

**Target length:** 2–3 minutes

**Format:** clean screen recording of the live scorer + SMooks voiceover. No camera or physical Mahjong set required.

## Canonical demonstration hand

Use this exact hand for the first video.

### Context

- Player Wind: **South**
- Prevailing Wind: **East**
- Winner: **Yes**
- Winning method: **ordinary discard**
- No Flowers or Seasons
- No Original Call
- Standard 1,000-point limit

### Hand

1. **Exposed Pung — 4 Bamboo**
   - 4 is a minor tile because suited tiles 2–8 are minor.
   - Exposed minor Pung = **2 points**.

2. **Concealed Pung — 7 Circles**
   - 7 is also a minor tile.
   - Concealed minor Pung = **4 points**.
   - This gives a clean visual contrast with the exposed 4 Bamboo Pung.

3. **Exposed Pung — Red Dragons**
   - Dragons are major/honour tiles.
   - Exposed major Pung = **4 points**.
   - Any Dragon Pung or Kong also gives **1 double**.

4. **Chow — 2, 3, 4 Characters**
   - Chows score **0 basic points**.
   - Including a Chow deliberately prevents the separate winner-only `No chows` double.

5. **Pair — 5 Bamboo**
   - This is an ordinary suited pair.
   - It scores **0 points**.

6. **Going Mah Jong**
   - A completed winning hand scores **20 points**.
   - Ordinary discard adds no extra winning-method points or double.

### Verified calculation

| Component | Value |
| --- | ---: |
| Exposed Pung of 4 Bamboo | 2 points |
| Concealed Pung of 7 Circles | 4 points |
| Exposed Pung of Red Dragons | 4 points |
| Chow of 2–3–4 Characters | 0 points |
| Pair of 5 Bamboo | 0 points |
| Going Mah Jong | 20 points |
| **Base points** | **30** |
| Red Dragon Pung | **1 double** |
| **Final score** | **60** |

Standard-hand calculation: `30 × 2 = 60`.

The hand deliberately mixes Bamboo, Circles and Characters, includes a Chow, and contains exposed sets, so it should not accidentally trigger Purity, one-suit-with-honours, no-Chows or fully-concealed winner doubles.

Before recording, enter this exact hand in the live scorer once and confirm the displayed breakdown still matches **30 points, 1 double, 60 final score**.

# Shot-by-shot screen recording and narration

## Shot 1 — Open the hand calculator

**Screen action**

Open `https://mahjong.smooks.co.uk/hand` in a clean desktop browser window.

Set:

- Player Wind = South
- Prevailing Wind = East
- Winner = Yes
- Winning method = ordinary discard

Do not enter any Flowers or Seasons.

**Voiceover**

> British Mahjong scoring can look complicated at first, but there are really two stages to the basic calculation. First, you add up the points in the hand. Then you apply any doubles. I’m going to build one ordinary hand and show you exactly where every part of the score comes from.

---

## Shot 2 — Add the exposed Pung of 4 Bamboo

**Screen action**

Create a **Pung**.

Set it to **Exposed**.

Choose **4 Bamboo**.

Pause on the completed set and, if visible without awkward scrolling, the score breakdown.

**Voiceover**

> First, this is a Pung of 4 Bamboo. In British Mahjong, suited tiles from 2 through 8 are called minor tiles. Four Bamboo is therefore a minor tile. An exposed Pung of minor tiles is worth 2 points, so our running total starts at 2.

---

## Shot 3 — Add the concealed Pung of 7 Circles

**Screen action**

Create a second **Pung**.

Set it to **Concealed**.

Choose **7 Circles**.

Pause on the set/breakdown.

**Voiceover**

> Next, I’m adding a Pung of 7 Circles. Seven is also a minor tile, but this Pung is concealed. A concealed minor Pung is worth 4 points instead of 2. So we now have 6 points from our first two Pungs.

---

## Shot 4 — Add the exposed Red Dragon Pung

**Screen action**

Create a third **Pung**.

Set it to **Exposed**.

Choose **Red Dragon**.

Pause long enough to show the point rule and, if the UI exposes it at this stage, the Dragon double.

**Voiceover**

> Our third set is a Pung of Red Dragons. Winds and Dragons are major tiles, so an exposed major Pung is worth 4 points. That takes our basic points from the sets to 10.

> But Dragons matter again later. A Pung or Kong of any Dragon also gives one double. The scorer keeps that separate from the basic points, which is useful because we haven’t applied the double yet.

---

## Shot 5 — Add the Chow of 2, 3, 4 Characters

**Screen action**

Create a **Chow**.

Choose **2 Characters** so the UI builds 2–3–4 Characters.

Pause on the completed Chow.

**Voiceover**

> Now I’m adding a Chow: 2, 3 and 4 Characters. Chows are useful for making the hand, but they score no basic points in British Mahjong. So our total stays at 10.

---

## Shot 6 — Add the pair of 5 Bamboo

**Screen action**

Create a **Pair**.

Choose **5 Bamboo**.

Pause on the completed hand.

If the winning-tile question appears because this is a discard win, select **5 Bamboo completing the pair**. This is only provenance for the completed hand; it does not add points or doubles in this example.

**Voiceover**

> Finally, we need a pair, so this hand has a pair of 5 Bamboo. Ordinary suited pairs don’t score anything, so we are still on 10 points from the tiles themselves.

---

## Shot 7 — Show the 20 points for going Mah Jong

**Screen action**

Scroll or move to the final score breakdown so **Going Mah-Jong — 20** is clearly visible.

**Voiceover**

> Because this is the winning hand, we now add 20 points for going Mah Jong. Ten points from the scoring sets, plus 20 for Mah Jong, gives us a basic score of 30 points.

On screen, if useful, briefly emphasise or zoom to:

**30 POINTS**

---

## Shot 8 — Apply the Dragon double

**Screen action**

Move focus to the doubles area showing the Red Dragon Pung double.

**Voiceover**

> Now we move to stage two: doubles. The Red Dragon Pung gives this hand one double. One double means we double the 30-point basic score once.

> Thirty doubled once is 60.

On screen, if useful, add a very restrained editor label:

`30 POINTS × 2 = 60`

---

## Shot 9 — Show final score and reasoning

**Screen action**

Leave the scorer on the final result with **60** and the breakdown visible.

**Voiceover**

> So this hand scores 60. The important thing isn’t memorising this particular hand. It’s the structure: work out the points first, then apply the doubles.

> Here, the exposed minor Pung gave us 2, the concealed minor Pung gave us 4, the Red Dragon Pung gave us another 4, the Chow and ordinary pair gave us nothing, and going Mah Jong added 20. That made 30 points. The Dragon Pung then gave one double, taking the final score to 60.

# Accuracy notes

Current project scoring rules used by this script:

- suited 2–8 = minor tiles;
- exposed minor Pung = 2;
- concealed minor Pung = 4;
- exposed major Pung = 4;
- Chows = 0;
- ordinary suited pair = 0;
- going Mah Jong = 20;
- Dragon Pung/Kong = one double;
- standard score = base points multiplied by `2^doubles`.

The canonical engineering source is `BMJA_RULES_REFERENCE.md` and the current scoring implementation/tests under `artifacts/mahjong-scorer/src/scoring/`.
