# Issue #72 source-quality notes

## Strong sources for public factual claims

- Project `BMJA_RULES_REFERENCE.md` for exactly what this scorer implements.
- Mahjong British Rules site for BMJA source lineage.
- Simon & Schuster / Simon & Schuster Australia publisher pages for Thompson & Maloney book scope.
- Penguin Australia for Max Robertson book status.
- Current Australian U3A / council / established community-group pages where they explicitly name their rules/books.

## Useful secondary source

Tom Sloper's Mahjong FAQ/Q&A material is useful for:

- terminology across Western/British/Australian traditions;
- published-rulebook cross-reference;
- the one-Chow Western rule;
- historical context.

Do not make Sloper the sole source for a contentious scoring-table value if a primary rulebook can be checked instead.

## Market-language evidence only

Current apps and informal discussion are useful for confirming that people actually use phrases such as:

- Western Mahjong
- Australian-style Western Mahjong
- British/Western Mahjong

They are not sufficient authority for exact scoring rules.

## Current strongest evidence points

1. Western Mahjong is used today by Australian U3A/community groups.
2. Thompson & Maloney are a major contemporary Australian reference.
3. Those groups may explicitly add local rules.
4. The Player's Companion has 120+ hands.
5. At least one current council-listed Western group uses 500/1000/1500/2000 hand-value categories.
6. The current BMJA scorer therefore must not be presented as automatically compatible with Australian Western play.
7. British/BMJA and Western/Australian traditions nevertheless share enough core concepts that a relationship/explainer page is genuinely useful.
