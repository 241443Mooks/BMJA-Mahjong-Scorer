# Research gaps

## Gap 1 — Actual frequency at live tables

Web evidence proves that scoring pauses, rules questions and teaching bottlenecks happen, but not how often across mature BMJA, family, Hong Kong or club play.

**Best next research:** observe and time 8–12 real sessions; log every pause for scoring, lookup, disagreement, explanation, state tracking or settlement.

## Gap 2 — My Table retention

The evidence supports custom rules and manual documentation but not repeated use of a digital profile.

**Best next research:** create profiles for 10–15 groups and follow 4–8 sessions; measure reopen/update/share/print/configure behaviour.

## Gap 3 — Physical-table willingness to pay

Commercial evidence comes mainly from bundled American Mah Jongg and isolated premium camera use.

**Best next research:** run a genuine paid pilot rather than a hypothetical survey.

## Gap 4 — BMJA-specific behaviour

Riichi is over-represented relative to modern British/BMJA tables.

**Best next research:** observe UK/BMJA tables and collect their score sheets, rule cards, local notes and designated-human workarounds.

## Gap 5 — Input-method race

Touch, camera and voice are solutions to the same entry-friction job.

**Best next research:** use the same physical hands and measure from hand-end to accepted score, including correction/confirmation time. Camera has enough evidence to test now; voice does not.

## Gap 6 — Which explanation moments create value

**Best next research:** log real questions and classify legality, scoring, settlement, terminology, ruleset difference, local-rule difference and teaching.

## Gap 7 — Shared-display behaviour

**Best next research:** compare central phone, tablet, cast/shared screen and individual devices in live play.
