# Gameplay basics

## A first game of British Mahjong, step by step

British Mahjong is played by building a hand while taking one tile and discarding one tile in turn.

For most hands, the aim is simple:

**four sets + one pair = Mah Jong**

A set can be a **Pung**, **Kong** or, in limited circumstances, a **Chow**. Some special hands use completely different patterns, but you do not need to learn those before playing your first game.

> **Beginner note**
> You do not need to memorise the scoring rules before you start playing. First learn the rhythm of the game: draw, decide, discard, and recognise when another player's discard can help you.

### Visual example

Use the shared SVG tile artwork to show one ordinary completed hand as:

- Pung
- Pung
- Chow
- Pung
- Pair

Label the groups rather than expecting the learner to identify them unaided.

# 1. Everyone has a Wind

At a four-player table, each player has a **seat Wind**:

- East
- South
- West
- North

These are not simply compass directions on the table. They are player roles that move as the game progresses.

**East is special:** East starts the hand and receives one extra tile in the deal so that they can make the first discard.

### Visual treatment

Show the four Wind SVG tiles in order:

**East → South → West → North**

Add a small caption:

> Play moves anti-clockwise through these player positions.

### Your Wind matters later

Your seat Wind can affect scoring. It also determines which Flower and Season are your own matching bonus tiles:

- East → Flower 1 / Season 1
- South → Flower 2 / Season 2
- West → Flower 3 / Season 3
- North → Flower 4 / Season 4

Link to the more visual Flower/Season explanation in the beginner/scoring guide rather than duplicating the full explanation here.

---

# 2. The deal

At the start of a hand:

- **East receives 14 playing tiles**
- **South, West and North receive 13 playing tiles each**

East has 14 because East begins the game by discarding one tile.

Flowers and Seasons are different. They are **bonus tiles**, not part of the normal 13-tile playing hand. If one is dealt to you, place it face-up and take a replacement tile from the **Kong box**.

If the replacement is another Flower or Season, lay that out too and replace it again.

### Visual treatment

Keep this very simple:

**East: 14 → discard → 13**

**Everyone else: 13**

Then show one Flower SVG moving out of the hand and a replacement tile coming from a small labelled **Kong box**.

> **Useful check**
> During ordinary play, if you count your playing tiles in groups of three, you should normally have one tile left over. Declared Kongs add an extra physical tile, so the number of visible tiles can be higher without the hand being wrong.

---

# 3. The rhythm of a turn

Most turns follow the same three steps:

1. **Draw one tile** from the live wall.
2. **Decide what to keep.**
3. **Discard one tile** face-up into the centre.

You can discard the tile you have just drawn or keep it and discard something else.

The discarded tile is normally finished with immediately. It only comes back into play if somebody is allowed to claim it straight away.

### Visual treatment

Use a compact three-step strip:

**Draw → Decide → Discard**

This should be one of the strongest visual anchors on the page.

---

# 4. The three ordinary kinds of set

## Pung

A **Pung** is three identical tiles.

### Visual

Show three identical SVG tiles, for example:

**7 Circles · 7 Circles · 7 Circles**

A Pung is one of the most common building blocks of a British Mahjong hand.

---

## Kong

A **Kong** is four identical tiles.

### Visual

Show four identical SVG tiles, for example four Red Dragons.

Because a Kong uses four physical tiles but fills one of the four set positions in the hand, declaring a Kong means you need a **replacement tile from the Kong box**.

> **Think of it this way**
> A Kong still counts as one set. The replacement tile keeps the rest of your hand the correct structural size.

---

## Chow

A **Chow** is three consecutive numbered tiles from the same suit.

### Visual

Show, for example:

**3 Bamboo · 4 Bamboo · 5 Bamboo**

Under the normal British rules used by this scorer, an ordinary hand may contain **no more than one Chow**.

A Chow also has a stricter claiming rule than a Pung or Kong: normally you may only claim a discard for a Chow when it is **your turn next**.

> **Beginner shortcut**
> Most of the sets you enter in the scorer will therefore be Pungs. Chows are more restricted, and Kongs are less common.

---

# 5. Claiming another player's discard

Sometimes the tile another player discards is exactly the tile you need.

A discard can be claimed immediately to complete an allowed set or to declare Mah Jong.

## Claiming a Pung

If a discard completes three identical tiles, you may call **“Pung!”**.

Place the completed Pung face-up in front of you. This is now an **exposed** set.

Because the claim interrupts the normal turn order, you then discard a tile and play continues from you.

## Claiming a Kong

If a discard completes four identical tiles, you may call **“Kong!”**.

Lay out the Kong, take a replacement tile from the Kong box, then continue your turn.

## Claiming a Chow

A discard can normally be claimed for a Chow only by the player whose turn would have been next.

Lay the Chow face-up, then discard a tile.

## Claiming Mah Jong

If the discarded tile completes your winning hand, call **“Mah Jong!”** instead.

There is no discard after the tile that completes Mah Jong.

### Visual treatment

A small decision diagram would work well:

**Someone discards a tile**

→ completes your Pung/Kong? **Claim it**

→ completes your Chow *and you are next to play*? **Claim it**

→ completes Mah Jong? **Declare Mah Jong**

→ otherwise **leave it**

Do not turn this into a rules-precedence table on the main beginner flow. More detailed precedence rules can sit inside an expandable “What if two people want the same discard?” note.

### Optional expandable note — What if two people want the same discard?

Mah Jong takes precedence over an ordinary set claim. If more than one player can declare Mah Jong from the same discard, precedence follows the normal anti-clockwise order from the discarder.

---

# 6. Concealed and exposed sets

A set is **concealed** when you make it using tiles from your original hand or tiles you draw from the wall, without claiming somebody else's discard to make that set.

A set is **exposed** when you claim another player's discard to complete it and lay it face-up on the table.

This matters because concealed sets can score differently from exposed sets.

### Visual treatment

Show the same Pung twice:

**Concealed Pung** — three tiles grouped behind/in the hand

**Exposed Pung** — same three tile SVGs laid openly in front

Do not make the learner memorise the scoring difference here. Add:

> The scorer keeps track of this because it affects points. You only need to tell it whether the set was exposed or concealed.

---

# 7. Flowers and Seasons interrupt the normal draw

Flowers and Seasons are bonus tiles. They do not stay in your playing hand.

Whenever you receive one:

1. place it face-up in front of you;
2. take a replacement tile from the Kong box;
3. if that replacement is another Flower or Season, repeat the process.

They can score points and doubles, but they do not help make your four sets and pair.

### Visual treatment

Use actual Flower and Season SVGs with visible names, not images alone.

For example:

**Flower 2 — Orchid (South)**

**Season 2 — Summer (South)**

Add a link/callout:

**Why do Flowers and Seasons match Winds? → Learn about matching bonus tiles**

---

# 8. What is the Kong box?

The **Kong box** is a small reserved group of tiles at the end of the wall.

Replacement tiles come from here when:

- you expose a Flower;
- you expose a Season; or
- you declare a Kong.

The ordinary turn-by-turn draw comes from the **live wall** instead.

### Visual treatment

A very simple wall diagram:

**Live wall** → normal draws

**Kong box** → replacement tiles only

Avoid teaching the full dice-and-wall-breaking procedure in the main flow. Put it behind an optional section for players who are physically setting up a table themselves.

---

# 9. How a hand ends

A hand usually ends when somebody declares **Mah Jong**.

For an ordinary hand, that normally means the player has completed:

**four sets + one pair**

The winning tile can come from the wall or from another player's discard.

Once Mah Jong is declared:

1. play stops;
2. all players lay out their hands;
3. the winner is scored first;
4. the other hands are scored;
5. payments are settled.

The scorer can handle those calculations for you.

### Visual treatment

Show:

**4 sets + pair → Mah Jong → score the table**

Add buttons/links at the end:

- **Score a game**
- **Score a hand**
- **Learn how scoring works**
- **Explore special hands**

# 10. What if nobody wins?

If the live wall runs out before anyone declares Mah Jong, the hand is drawn.

A hand can also be drawn if a replacement tile is required but the Kong box is empty.

For a beginner-facing page, stop there. The **goulash** is a British rules continuation with additional rules and should be introduced separately rather than added to the first-game flow.

---

# 11. How the Winds move between hands

This is one of the parts that feels complicated until you see the pattern.

## If East wins

East **stays East** and the seat Winds do not move.

## If somebody other than East wins

The seat Winds move on:

**South becomes East**

**West becomes South**

**North becomes West**

**East becomes North**

## If the hand is drawn

East stays East.

### Visual treatment

Use the four Wind SVGs in a circular/step diagram rather than a paragraph.

Add a beginner callout:

> **You do not become East simply because you won.** East moves to the player who was South when the previous East does not win.

---

# 12. Seat Wind and prevailing Wind are different

Your **seat Wind** identifies your current player position.

The **prevailing Wind** (also called the Wind of the round) applies to the whole table and changes more slowly.

The game begins with East as the prevailing Wind.

The prevailing Wind changes after the original East player has moved through the other seat Winds and becomes East again.

You do not need to calculate this while using the game scorer — the app can track it as the game progresses.

### Visual treatment

Use two clearly separate badges:

**Your seat Wind: South**

**Prevailing Wind: East**

Caption:

> They can be the same, but they are not the same thing.

Do not imply that the prevailing-Wind player starts the hand. **East always starts**, regardless of the prevailing Wind.

---

# 13. The five things to remember for your first game

Keep this as a strong closing summary card.

1. **Build four sets and a pair.**
2. **On your turn: draw one, discard one.**
3. **Pungs and Kongs are matching tiles; a Chow is a three-tile run.**
4. **Flowers, Seasons and Kongs get replacement tiles from the Kong box.**
5. **East starts, and East stays East if East wins.**

Then add:

> That is enough to start playing. The scorer can help with the parts that are harder to remember.

# Rules/source notes

This content follows the British rules reference already used by the project, including the published material at `mahjongbritishrules.wordpress.com` on **Preparing to play**, **Playing the game**, and associated questions pages.

Important rules represented here include:

- East receives 14 tiles; the other players receive 13.
- Flowers and Seasons are laid out and replaced from the Kong box.
- East makes the first discard.
- Ordinary turns proceed by obtaining one tile and discarding one tile.
- A normal Mah Jong is normally four sets plus a pair.
- Only the next player may normally claim a discard for a Chow; Pung/Kong claims can interrupt normal turn order.
- Claimed sets are exposed; sets built without claiming a discard are concealed.
- Declared Kongs receive a replacement tile from the Kong box.
- East remains East if East wins; if East loses, the seat Winds rotate; a drawn hand does not move East.
- The prevailing Wind is distinct from the seat Wind and does not determine who starts.

The site remains an independent project and should not present itself as an official BMJA publication.
