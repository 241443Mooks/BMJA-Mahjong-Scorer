## Inventory boundary

Babcock mixes ordinary arithmetic scoring with named bonuses, doubling conditions, special scores and optional hands.

For 251A1, **primitive score-table cells are not counted as separate Encyclopaedia entries**. Examples excluded from the finite concept count include:

- exposed/concealed ordinary pung point rows;
- terminal/honour pung and kong base-point rows;
- pair base-point rows;
- the base 20 points for completing Mah-Jongg.

Those belong to a future executable Classical scoring profile, not to the Encyclopaedia concept count.

The counted corpus therefore contains **24 reference-worthy source-local scoring concepts**: named/event bonuses, doubling conditions, standard special scores and optional hands/treatments.

## Why Babcock matters to the Encyclopaedia

Babcock is useful not merely because it is old. It exposes three different layers that the public Encyclopaedia must eventually keep distinct:

1. **primitive scoring mechanics** — base points for sets/pairs;
2. **reference-worthy concepts** — bonuses, patterns and special hands;
3. **table configuration** — optional scores agreed before play.

That distinction should inform later 251B data modelling without forcing the future rules runtime to mirror this document structure.
