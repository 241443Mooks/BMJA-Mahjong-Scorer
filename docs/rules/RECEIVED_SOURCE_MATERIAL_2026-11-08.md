## Why this record exists

An in-person Mahjong group visit exposed two practical, player-led needs:

1. A **simple, immediately accessible scoring table** for the *selected exact rules profile*: what each set/pair/bonus is worth, relevant doublings, limits, winning conditions, and who pays whom. Some values are already on the site but buried inside longer explanations.
2. A **legible, printable special-hands reference**, suitable for equal access at the table. A player offered to review a prototype. An original design should be tested for clarity, legibility and usefulness in actual play.

These are product observations, not proof that the scorer is wrong or that any particular historical rule should be implemented. Keep the scoring reference distinct from deeper profile reports, the Encyclopaedia and comparative research. Keep the printable reference selected-profile-specific and derived from reviewed legal-to-publish information.

No names or participant contact details are recorded here.

## Source BELL-1978 — R. C. Bell, *Discovering Mah-jong*

- Author: R. C. Bell.
- Publisher/series: Shire Publications, *Discovering* series, no. 222.
- First published: 1976. Second edition: 1978. Photographed copy identifies a 1979 reprint.
- ISBN printed in copy: 0 85263 444 7.
- Evidence class: **dated authored historical/multi-tradition reference**, authoritative for what this edition describes, **not** a current governing-body rulebook.
- Scope in contents: Chinese, Japanese and American Mah-jong; two-, three- and five-player arrangements; cards, history and bibliography.

### Selected page locators and observations

| Locator in supplied photographs | What to check or use | Safe current treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pp. 3–5 | Terminology, 136-tile base set and optional bonus tiles, tallies and table procedure | Historical explanation; do not infer modern profile values |
| Chinese chapter, photographed scoring section (printed page number not visible in these shots) | Minor/major Pungs and Kongs, exposed/concealed differences, pairs, winning bonus and doubles | Compare numeric facts with BMJA and other *named* profiles before promoting |
| pp. 14–15 | Bell's named special/limit hands, 2,000-point stated limit, settlement, round progression and penalties | Treat as Bell's historical Chinese account, **not** the BMJA limit |
| pp. 17–19 | Strategy and defensive play; holding Winds/Dragons, options late in the wall | Editorial teaching leads only; not rule authority |
| pp. 20–21 | Flowers and Seasons: four points per tile, matching seat-Wind doubling, complete Flower/Season set treatment; illustrative extreme-score example | Corroborating source-local mechanics; check exact stacking/cap before comparing with scorer |
| pp. 21–23 | Distinguishes folklore about ancient origins from historic evidence; cites Stewart Culin's 1893 description of related sets | History research leads; do not repeat origin myths as fact |
| Japanese chapter, photographed introduction and pp. 34–35 transition | Historical terminology, winner payments, declared ready hand, seven pairs, 'Furi Tem' description | Historical comparison only, not a specification for EMA 2025 |
| p. 35 | Bell's historical American account and non-modern scoring conventions | **Do not equate** with present-day NMJL annual-card American Mahjong |

| p. 46 (partial) | Appendix examples of later special hands | **Incomplete photograph; not a finite, verified catalogue** |

Noteworthy **source-local** comparison candidates: Bell's 2,000-point limit versus the 1,000-point BMJA baseline; the handling of Flowers/Seasons and cumulative doubles; named historical special hands; and the distinction between Chinese all-player settlement and the Japanese winner-centred presentation. Any proposed equivalence or runtime discrepancy requires an exact-page reading, the current authority, and executable evidence.

## Source PRITCHARD-2013 — David Pritchard, *Play and Win Mah-jong*

- Author: David Pritchard.
- Series/publisher: *Teach Yourself*, Hodder & Stoughton / Hachette UK.
- First UK publication identified: 2001. Photographed edition: 2013.
- Evidence class: **dated authored, multi-tradition instructional/reference book**; useful for comparative claims, **not** an association rulebook or universal modern profile.
- Available evidence: selected pages and chapter opening photographs, **not a complete transcription**.

### Selected page locators and observations

| Locator in supplied photographs | What to check or use | Safe current treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chapter 4 basic-score table (page number not visible in photo) | Direct, beginner-friendly table for exposed/concealed Chows, minor/major Pungs and Kongs, pairs, and Flower/Season bonuses | Useful *presentation precedent*; create our **own** table from governed profile facts rather than reproducing book layout |
| Chapter 5 heading | Announces 18 special hands and 12 optional special hands | A *claim about the book's contents*, **not** 30 audited entries or an addition to the source inventory |
| pp. 110–113 | Two-, three-, five- and six-player alternatives; different walls, seats, rotations, Chow and settlement choices | Explicit variant candidates; not one canonical 'three-player Mahjong' |
| pp. 146–149 | Dated Japanese discussion: patterns, Riichi, Dora, red fives, Furiten and abortive draws | Historical/teaching cross-check against **EMA Riichi 2025**; follow the pinned modern source for runtime |
| pp. 152–153 | Optional changes: bonus tiles, cleared/single-suit restriction, one-Chow rule, no-Chow rule, minimum double, winner-only payments | Strong questionnaire/comparison *dimensions*, **not** universal or default values |
| p. 156 | Taiwanese 16-tile (five sets plus a pair), and varied Vietnamese, Thai and Malaysian tile/bonus arrangements | Use to investigate named profiles; do not describe all countries as having one uniform ruleset |
| p. 157 | Unusual/extra tiles may have set-specific uses | Tile-variation Encyclopaedia lead, subject to specific-set evidence |

Pritchard's basic score table includes the familiar minor/major exposed/concealed values and four-point Flowers/Seasons. Similar numbers across traditions **do not** prove they have the same winning conditions, hand grammar, doubles, settlement or legal special hands.

For Riichi, preserve the explicit distinction between this book's discussion and `docs/rules/riichi/`'s version-pinned EMA 2025 authority. In particular do not import its historical fan/double descriptions or optional red-fives practice into the EMA 2025 profile.
